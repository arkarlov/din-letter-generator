# Envelope Addressing and Layout

This document summarizes practical envelope-addressing rules for domestic and
international letter mail sent through Deutsche Post. It focuses on ordinary
rectangular envelopes without a window. Special services, large-format mail,
and country-specific destination rules may require a different layout.

## 1. Envelope Side and Address Blocks

Write on the front (address side) only. Keep the sender and recipient addresses
separate from postage, service labels, decorative printing, and machine-readable
areas.

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Sender address                         Franking zone     │
│ Name                                    (top right)       │
│ Street and house number                                  │
│ Postal code and city                                      │
│                                                          │
│                                  Recipient address        │
│                                  Name / company           │
│                                  Street and number        │
│                                  Postal code and city      │
│                                  Country, if international│
│                                                          │
│                         Keep the lower-right coding area  │
│                         clear for postal automation       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The diagram is schematic, not to scale. The intended orientation is the normal
reading orientation of the envelope, regardless of how a printer feeds it.

## 2. Domestic Mail

For domestic letters, Deutsche Post recommends this general placement:

- Sender address: upper left; include it so an undeliverable letter can be
  returned.
- Recipient address: lower right.
- Stamp or other valid postage: upper right.
- Write only on the front. Avoid labels or printed designs that could be
  mistaken for postal marks.

Address typography and margins:

- Align address lines to the left, with no blank lines inside the address.
- Use one font, font size, and consistent line spacing within an address block.
- Use dark text on a light, plain background. Avoid frames and highlighting.
- Keep at least 15 mm from the left, right, and bottom edges, and at least
  40 mm from the top edge of the address area.
- Provide the sender address in full and make it machine-readable; Deutsche
  Post says the same legibility rules apply to the sender address.

Common rectangular formats accepted as Standard or Compact letters include
DIN long (110 x 220 mm), C6 (114 x 162 mm), and B6 (125 x 176 mm). Product
eligibility also depends on the current length, width, thickness, weight, and
aspect-ratio limits; format name alone does not determine postage.

## 3. International Mail

International letters use the same basic front-side layout, with additional
rules for the destination address:

- Write the recipient address in Latin characters. If the destination uses a
  different script, add that version as supplementary information.
- Do not put a country abbreviation or ISO code before the postal code or city.
- Write the destination city in uppercase, preferably in the destination
  country's language.
- Put the destination country on the last address line, in uppercase, using
  German, French, or English.
- Keep the address lines together without blank lines.

For business mail sent through the international products described in the
Deutsche Post handling brochure, the sender section generally requires one
German sender address. Product-specific exceptions apply, including certain
individually franked private-customer letters; check the current product terms
before relying on this rule.

## 4. Automated Processing Zones

The international handling brochure provides a measured layout for standard
formats without a window. These measurements are useful when reserving space for
an international envelope design:

| Zone                   | Placement and guidance                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sender zone            | Upper left; sender and optional service notes go here.                                                                                          |
| Franking zone          | Upper right, 74 x 40 mm; reserve for postage marks.                                                                                             |
| Recipient reading zone | Recipient address; longest line no more than 100 mm, with at least 15 mm from both side edges. Keep a 3 mm clear/light area around the address. |
| Coding zone            | Lower right, 150 x 15 mm; leave blank for the postal barcode.                                                                                   |

The domestic address page independently specifies the 15 mm bottom margin; the
150 x 15 mm coding-zone dimensions above are from the international automation
layout. Treat the coding zone as reserved space when designing for automated
processing, but do not assume that every country's equipment uses identical
dimensions.

Other text, service labels, or priority markings must not intrude into the
recipient reading area. Do not print anything that could be confused with
postage or postal service labels. For standard and compact international mail,
rectangular formats are required; large and maxi formats have separate layout
guidance and are outside this document's scope.

## 5. Window Envelopes

The recipient address must remain fully visible and readable through the window
even if the contents shift. The window should be clear, non-reflective, firmly
attached, and flush with the envelope surface. Ensure that no unrelated letter
text or graphics can appear in the window when the contents move. Deutsche Post
recommends envelope windows conforming to DIN 680 for international mail.

## 6. Cards with a Dividing Line

Do not apply the card layout with a dividing line to ordinary envelopes. In the
international brochure, the 74 mm measurement from the right edge describes the
reading-zone boundary for cards with a dividing line. For standard envelopes
without a window, 74 x 40 mm describes the franking zone instead.

## 7. Priority and Postage

Postage and priority are separate:

- A stamp, Internetmarke, or other permitted franking pays for the mail item.
- `PRIORITY / Prioritaire / Luftpost` indicates priority transport for
  international mail; it is not postage by itself.
- The priority mark may be placed above the recipient address or in another
  designated clear area. Keep it outside the address block and reserved postal
  zones.
- Keep stamps and other franking in the upper-right franking zone. Do not scale,
  crop, or cover required parts of a purchased digital franking mark.

## 8. Format and Printer Checks

Before printing, verify the envelope's actual dimensions and that the printer
supports feeding that envelope size. Print at 100% scale with resizing disabled;
check that postage, address margins, and reserved zones remain within the
physical envelope. Postal size eligibility and printer feed compatibility are
separate checks.
